[AUT-374](https://linear.app/auto-core-platform/issue/AUT-374) is the Epic 7 source of truth for customer communication permissions. Transactional order messages are **not consent-based**. ## Purpose, lawful basis and channel matrix

Separate **message class**, **specific purpose**, **GDPR basis**, **permission status**, **delivery preference** and **address validity**. A single green “consent” checkbox cannot represent all six.

| Class / purpose | Basis and policy |
| --- | --- |
| TRANSACTIONAL / ORDER_COMMUNICATION: estimate, decision confirmation, ready-for-pickup | Contract/pre-contract necessity, GDPR Art 6(1)(b), with original order/recipient context. No marketing consent or soft opt-in required. Address confirmation records supplied contact information, not permission for advertising. |
| TRANSACTIONAL / INVOICE_DELIVERY | Applicable contract/legal obligation context, Art 6(1)(b)/(c). Retention obligation is not permission to email an unwanted/invalid address; phone/print remains available. |
| MARKETING / PICKERL_REMINDER, SERVICE_REMINDER, REVIEW_REQUEST, SEASONAL_OFFERS, NEWSLETTER | Separate granular purpose and channel. Explicit consent (Art 6(1)(a), TKG §174(3)) or fully evidenced soft opt-in with separate GDPR legitimate-interest assessment. Reminders/review requests are product-classified marketing by default, not a legal finding that all such communications must always be advertising. |
| INTERNAL in-app advisor event | Existing tenant staff workflow, not customer marketing; customer channel preferences do not suppress internal operational alerts. |

Email and SMS are separate. Phone/print are manual service alternatives, not new marketing channels. WhatsApp/OEM sharing/profiling/tracking pixels and campaigns are out of scope. Never bundle marketing into a transactional template, signature, repair agreement or privacy-notice acknowledgement. No pre-ticked marketing controls; refusal/revocation cannot reduce service or prevent the order. Fresh specific consent after refusal requires new affirmative evidence, never an advisor toggle or import overriding an objection.

Soft opt-in is **not consent**. All four TKG conditions must be evidenced: contact obtained with a sale/service; own similar products/services; a clear free refusal opportunity at collection and every message; no prior refusal including applicable ECG-list rejection. Model missing/unknown as false. Imported addresses, a historical invoice alone, silence, address confirmation or a current “unsubscribe” footer cannot prove the missing collection-time conditions. Art 6(1)(f) assessment additionally records purpose/necessity/balancing; TKG exception alone is not a GDPR legal basis. Re-evaluate similarity for each proposed message; a tyre-service relationship does not authorise arbitrary partner offers.

## Proposed data model (expand-only)

**Controller identity is mandatory:** `controller_legal_entity_id` identifies the workshop legal entity whose communication is being recorded, with composite `[tenant_id,controller_legal_entity_id] -> LegalEntity[tenant_id,id]` FK. Tenant.legalEntities is one-to-many: the schema supports more than one controller company per tenant. Freeze the controller on every permission event, communication state, notice receipt, DPA acceptance, marketing objection, opt-out token and outbox decision. Address identities may stay tenant/customer-wide, never positive permissions. All model keys/scopes below include this controller dimension unless explicitly classified as transport-only.

Resolve sender controller from the authorised order/document's persisted seller/legal-entity evidence, not the current user/site switcher or a customer preference. Marketing consumers supply an authorised explicit controller and its own relationship evidence. Missing/ambiguous scope fails `COMMUNICATION_CONTROLLER_REQUIRED` before enqueue/send; A's grant or soft-opt-in sale cannot authorise B. Authenticated controller actions require the stated role plus active-site membership for that controller; body ids alone never authorise. UI names the controller covered by each choice. A site seller change never retargets existing consent or queued messages.

Required controller-scoped schema keys: CustomerConsent gains controller FK; CustomerCommunicationState unique key is `[tenant_id,controller_legal_entity_id,customer_id]`; CustomerPrivacyNoticeReceipt and TenantDpaAcceptance carry the same controller FK, and a group AVV acceptance enumerates represented entities with separate per-controller receipts and authority evidence. NotificationOutbox/token/decision rows persist this controller. Supersedes-event links and cached proof projections must match tenant/controller/customer/contact/purpose. Controller-specific erasure/restriction remains in that controller state. A separate explicit tenant/customer-wide restriction (for a verified all-controller request or offboarding) dominates every projection; add nullable Customer.processing_restricted_at and restriction_scope metadata in the erasure implementation. A request to A alone cannot erase or restrict B's independently lawful relationship; shared customer-shell deletion waits for every controller dependency.

ContactSuppression distinguishes CONTROLLER scope (required controller FK for marketing objection/no-electronic-contact) from TENANT_TRANSPORT (null controller allowed only for HARD_BOUNCE/INVALID_ADDRESS). Controller objections apply across duplicate customers only for that controller/address; transport invalidity may block all tenant sends but never grant permission. Reconsent for A cannot clear B's objection or address transport invalidity. Dispatch's per-tenant/address mutex may serialise the broader transport fence while authorising each attempt against its exact controller state. Controller withdrawal cancels only covered controller messages; tenant erasure/invalidity covers all relevant rows.

Legacy migration safety: `contact_email_ok=true` authorises a CONTRACT source event only if the original controller is unambiguous (single-controller tenant or recorded original order/source evidence). Multiple companies plus a tenant-wide flag do not create a grant for each company: retain the observation and require new controller-specific advisor confirmation before sending. Migration idempotency includes controller; no historical collection date/consent is fabricated. After cutover, the legacy boolean is a usable projection only for a single unambiguous controller; multi-controller clients read named controller state. Legacy true writes lacking a resolvable controller fail, not update all companies. Adding a new legal entity never inherits old derived true flags. This limits the general migration rules below; ambiguous E3 rows show confirmation required rather than silently continuing.

Controller-level consent/notice/evidence is not automatically visible tenant-wide: the requesting staff member needs the stated role and an active site of that controller, and linked source-order evidence retains its own site guard. A customer may have separate valid service contacts for A and B; a public opt-out token for A can mutate only A, even if both companies share tenant/customer/address. Public token requests never accept a replacement controller id. Re-subscription is explicit purpose-scoped evidence for that same controller; preserve all other controllers' and purposes' objections.

All new tenant rows have UUID id, tenant_id, timestamps and `[tenant_id,id]` uniqueness; customer/contact/actor/evidence FKs are composite tenant references. Actor uses authenticated TenantMember, not mechanic Employee. Customer/address identity is tenant-wide; permission state is controller-specific, with the additional controller/site guards above. Order/outbox/document reads remain authorised-site scoped. No site switch may make another site's order available through consent evidence. Evidence readers return redacted metadata unless explicitly authorised to see the linked evidence; source IDs alone grant no access.

GRANTED with CONTRACT/LEGAL_OBLIGATION means “source recorded for this transactional channel”, not GDPR consent. UI labels it accordingly. REVOKED event has `revoked_at=occurred_at` and references affected event/scope; the old grant's revoked_at is **never updated**. Effective state is the latest server sequence for this contact/purpose, plus stronger current objections/suppression/restriction. Client-supplied occurred_at cannot reorder or backdate away a withdrawal. No update/delete API for ledger entries. Material correction is a new superseding event. Erasure/retention jobs may remove expired evidence/events under the controlled lifecycle below; append-only means no ordinary business mutation, not permanent storage.

Exact optional consent text, notice, language and purpose/channel are snapshotted; i18n follows AUT-575/587. Do not reuse today's translated wording as proof of an old choice. Evidence excludes password/payment data/unrelated notes. Use minimal keyed IP/device evidence only where justified; no raw fingerprinting. JSON evidence fields have explicit schema/length/type limits and no arbitrary scripts/URLs. Public tokens stored as hashes, never in ledger/outbox logs.

## Effective-state and migration rules

1. Incomplete/no ledger = marketing denied. A transactional point can be usable only when the customer supplied the current address for order use, the message has necessary order/document context and all suppression/preference/domain guards pass.
2. Contact change creates/selects a different point and starts unconfirmed for transactional use, with no marketing grants. Old points/evidence remain historical and cannot authorise the new address. No normalisation-based broadening to another mailbox. Switching back does not silently revive a revoked/invalid point.
3. Customer merge/import must not choose the more permissive duplicate: preserve separate event history, apply address-level suppression and current objections first, require explicit supported reconciliation. Unverified imports create no marketing grant/soft-opt-in. No cross-tenant consent transfer.

Expand-only migration from `Customer.contact_email_ok`:

- Keep old field/routes readable/writable during rollout. For each valid current email + true flag with unambiguous original controller as required above, idempotently create a LEGACY_MIGRATION GRANTED / EMAIL / ORDER_COMMUNICATION / CONTRACT event bound to that controller and exact point. Mark `recorded_at=migration time`, original collection/wording **unknown**, and evidence “observed legacy contact_email_ok=true”; do not fabricate consent, original date, IP or signed notice. False/missing/invalid email yields no positive record. Never create marketing permission.
- Add transactional INVOICE_DELIVERY source only when its original domain record establishes that communication purpose; do not infer every legal obligation authorises emailing. Existing invoice workflow must retain its basis/recipient checks.
- Deploy tables/dual-write first, batch backfill with resumable unique migration keys, then parity check and switch readers. All subsequent customer create/patch, intake/address confirmation/import and hard-bounce paths append the matching source/revocation event and compatibility flag update in **one transaction** under the state-row lock. After cutover ledger is authority; the old flag is only a single-unambiguous-controller EMAIL/ORDER_COMMUNICATION projection; multi-controller clients use the named projection. No path may set true while ledger says withdrawn/invalid/restricted.
- Old binaries that write the flag directly cannot coexist with ledger-only readers: complete deployment fencing (disable old writer revision / route traffic to upgraded dual-writers) before cutover, and run reconciliation. Backfill cannot overwrite a more recent withdrawal. Keep the old column until a separately planned contract-removal release; no destructive migration here.
- AUT-372 adds `contact_sms_ok` only as the equivalent transactional projection. Existing arbitrary Customer.phone remains original data; a validated SMS contact point is separate. It adds no marketing_consent booleans. E3 email remains usable where controller/source evidence is established; ambiguous sends require named-controller confirmation; inability to establish ledger parity must hold cutover, never silently grant permissions.

## Sending guard and outbox contract

Every template registry entry has immutable `message_class`, exact purpose, channel, version and required context. Unknown/unclassified templates fail closed. E3 templates remain TRANSACTIONAL only. Transactional lint/semantic review rejects offers, upsells, coupons, review links, reminder promotions and tracking content; fixtures plus an allowlisted template manifest supplement lint, which cannot prove prose has no advertising. Marketing uses separate templates and provider sender stream/configuration; no automatic downgrade to transactional when guard denies.

Implement a shared pure decision contract `evaluateContactPermission(context) -> {allowed,reason,event_id,state_revision,checked_at,proof_refs}`. Tenant-aware caller supplies frozen sender controller_legal_entity_id plus same-controller contact/ledger/relationship/suppression/notice/DPA configuration, template context and external-list result; client cannot choose a favourable message_class or basis. Evaluate on enqueue **and again before each provider handoff**, including retries, admin replay, advisor resend, token refresh and batch/reminder processing. Snapshot controller, recipient address and content on enqueue; never swap controller or address at retry. An updated address requires a fresh authorised send/new occurrence.

Guard order:

1. Existing tenant active, authorised order/site, role, provider configured/flag/allowlist, SideEffectGuard and template integrity checks. Staff resends remain domain-authorised; TECH/DEMO cannot bypass send restrictions. Read consent does not confer send rights.
2. Processing restriction/erasure, invalid address/hard bounce, no-electronic-contact preference and applicable suppression deny. Do not send an “opt-in request” to bypass a refusal.
3. TRANSACTIONAL: require current contact source for relevant purpose, necessary order/document relationship, no advertising and recipient match; no marketing-consent/ECG dependency. Channel refusal means phone/print/manual service follow-up, not “order cannot proceed”. Manual phone is not permission for promotional calls.
4. MARKETING: require exact current purpose/channel consent OR all soft-opt-in facts plus per-message own-similar-products assessment. Any unresolved prior objection/revocation dominates. Require verified current address, approved text/template, fresh applicable ECG result, correct sender stream/identity and a working free opt-out. Missing proof/config/list result suppresses, never falls through to old flag.

RTR ECG check: for **marketing EMAIL**, product policy requires a fresh CLEAR address/domain result even for explicit opt-in (deliberately stricter than relying on the statutory exception alone). Cache at most 24h, recheck every attempt when expired; positive HIT suppresses, UNKNOWN/unavailable/stale suppresses with `ECG_CHECK_UNAVAILABLE`, no provider call. Persist check time/list/version/result and hashed address, not the raw entire list. RTR's documented list concerns email; never claim an email lookup checks E.164. SMS records ECG applicability `NOT_APPLICABLE_EMAIL_LIST` with the documented policy version, but still requires permission, own refusal checks and per-message SMS opt-out; no fabricated CLEAR for a phone number. Real RTR adapter/access terms are an implementation prerequisite to marketing email activation, not a blocker to building fake-tested ledger/guards. CI never queries RTR.

Permission mutations lock the tenant/controller/customer state row, increment revision and cancel/suppress outstanding queued messages covered by withdrawal; worker final check shares a per-tenant/address dispatch fence with suppression/revocation so duplicate customers cannot race. Acquire fence, reread current state/proof and invoke the provider only if allowed. Revoke/objection uses the same fence and commits before any later handoff; attempts already handed to a provider cannot be recalled. A bounded in-flight provider attempt may finish before the withdrawal commits; show pending/confirmed withdrawal accurately and never promise recall. Provider timeout stays within existing 15s contract, no long database transaction across HTTP; use an explicit bounded dispatch mutex/fence in the owning plan. Record handoff/state revision and audit the outcome. A stale lease/replay cannot reuse yesterday's allowed decision.

Blocked row becomes SUPPRESSED with safe stable reason (e.g. MARKETING_PERMISSION_MISSING, CONTACT_WITHDRAWN, CONTACT_INVALID, PROCESSING_RESTRICTED, ECG_LISTED). Existing replay of FAILED rows must not bypass current guard or resurrect SUPPRESSED rows. Correcting a contact or receiving a new grant does not automatically send old suppressed marketing. Provider ACCEPTED remains acceptance, not delivery; timeout-unknown behaviour preserves existing duplicate prevention. A hard bounce appends invalidity/suppression for the **snapshotted recipient** even if customer email has since changed, and resets only the matching current transactional projection. Lifting HARD_BOUNCE needs verified correction/reconfirmation, not marketing opt-in alone.

## Opt-out, objection and customer interfaces

Customer profile/intake separates “E-Mail/SMS für diesen Auftrag”, preferred service channel, privacy notice supplied, and optional channel/purpose marketing choices. All optional marketing controls start off; refusal is equally easy, available later without login, payment or completing a survey. Don't make repair/save/signature depend on marketing choice. Draft service preferences can autosave at 750ms, but affirmative consent/withdrawal actions submit explicitly with exact text/version and persistent confirmed/error state; never show Saved before server commit. Prefer shared UI/accessibility/status/query patterns, translated labels, no raw key/body display.

Proposed authenticated routes (new implementing DTO/controller changes regenerate OpenAPI and web types): every controller-specific request includes an explicit controller_legal_entity_id validated against authorised site/controller context; related order/document evidence must match it. Reads return only that controller projection, never all companies by default. Public tokens resolve their frozen controller server-side, not from body input.

| `GET /api/customers/:id/communication-preferences` | Tenant-scoped effective current points/purposes and revision; OWNER/ADMIN/SALES. TECH/DEMO denied ledger/evidence access. No unrelated site-owned order evidence. |
| `POST /api/customers/:id/communication-events` | Explicit event request with expected_revision, idempotency_key, contact point, purpose/status, approved text_version, source and typed evidence. OWNER/ADMIN/SALES can record a customer's evidenced choice, never manufacture consent. Reject unsupported status/basis combinations, foreign points and missing proof. Server sets actor/sequence/recorded time and appends transactionally. |
| `PATCH /api/customers/:id/service-contact-preferences` | Revisioned channel/fallback/no-electronic-contact choice; updates state and appends affected transactional source/refusal events. Marketing unaffected except a broader electronic-contact objection can suppress it too. |

Marketing messages include an opaque, scoped public opt-out token (32 random bytes, hash stored) bound to tenant/controller_legal_entity_id, contact fingerprint and **marketing-all-purposes for that controller/channel**, not a public customer id. Public GET displays a minimal confirmation/action and no customer/order/consent history; GET never mutates. Public POST withdraws all that controller's marketing on that address/channel idempotently and appends an objection, stops queued eligible sends and returns generic success even for replay/unknown token; invalid token performs no mutation. No account login or marketing confirmation email required. Rate-limit, constant response shape, no third-party analytics/referrer leakage, no token logging. Add standards-compatible one-click POST unsubscribe headers for email as a separate signed/scoped POST, not a destructive GET followed by mail scanners. Tokens in already sent messages remain valid until their proof retention expiry; replaced/expired links offer a manual controller opt-out contact with generic response, not a forced login. Public route/worker narrowly allowlisted under existing system-Prisma/security conventions; never unauthenticated arbitrary tenant/customer writes.

Consent collection via public form needs a verified contact-ownership step (double opt-in for email as product policy), purpose-specific text and minimal evidence; receipt/confirmation messages are non-promotional, rate-limited and never repeated to suppressed addresses. Confirming an email only proves address control, not blanket marketing consent. A new consent after a marketing-all objection must expressly request resubscription for the chosen purpose; log proof/revision, retain old objection history and never remove refusal for other purposes. SMS opt-out is AUT-372's provider-supported inbound STOP or a short signed HTTPS link plus valid free controller contact in every message; no claimed inbound STOP support unless the provider supports it. No channel activates without a working unsubscribe mechanism.

Manual “stop emailing” requests default to a broad no-electronic-email contact preference; advisor can clarify scope without making the customer continue electronic contact. A marketing-only opt-out does not block necessary service messages to an otherwise valid chosen address; a service-email refusal does. Art 21 marketing objection stops that processing; lawfully retained invoices remain restricted and deliverable by print. Existing order workflow continues, with internal “Kontakt per Telefon/Brief klären” task/warning rather than auto-sending by another channel without a source record.

## Consent evidence retention and erasure

Active consent/soft-opt-in proof stays only while relied on and necessary; on withdrawal or terminal invalidity close the proof chain with an immutable cessation event. Retain relevant proof until **withdrawal/unsubscribe +3 calendar years** (same date, leap day clamps), with retain_until/legal hold in the retention engine; this is AUT-374's engineering limitation-policy default, not a universal statutory period. No year-end rounding for consent proof. Grant rows are not edited: keep their per-row storage envelope metadata separate, and project a proof-chain retain_until from the cessation event. Do not destroy old text/evidence while an unexpired linked proof chain still needs it. Hold changes are audited controlled metadata, not rewritten customer choice.

Suppression is minimal HMAC/channel/controller/reason, not continued marketing. Keep it while the same identifier is held/used for outreach and otherwise up to the proof deadline, with documented necessity review; do not expire it while importing/retaining contact lists could silently revive refused marketing. HMAC/key rotation preserves matching across current versions without cross-tenant lookup. Deleting marketing evidence never generates a new permission; imports default denied, restored suppression/erasure log reapplied before any workers run.

Erase unnecessary contact/evidence/notes promptly after verified erasure request; customers with retained invoices get TOMBSTONED/RESTRICTED processing state and no operational marketing/search/export use. Keep minimal FK-compatible customer shell plus encrypted/restricted evidence needed for claims/retention; do not erase legally required invoice snapshot/PDF recipient fields or recalculate immutable documents. Retain those until per-document retain_until (existing invoice default 31 December of document year +7, plus hold/overrides), then a second pass deletes/anonymises shell/evidence once all dependencies expire. A restricted data-access path for OWNER/ADMIN finance/privacy duty may retrieve retained originals for legal purpose; ordinary CRM/search/API/MCP/agent/report queries exclude tombstones except explicit authorised retention views. No API “force delete” around existing relation guards.

Retention jobs inventory customer-related data in live rows, audit/payload/evidence blobs, notifications, registration intake, photos/voice, exports, cached search and backup rotation. Purge unrelated PII even if an invoice prevents customer deletion; never hold all workshop media indefinitely because an invoice exists. Existing outbox body 90-day-from-creation purge remains; metadata <=3 years is separate from consent proof and document legal hold. Versioned rules identify necessary exceptions/holds instead of blanket indefinite retention. For genuine correction of invoice data use existing correction/credit workflows; erasure never silently edits financial snapshots.

Tenant offboarding: stop sends/access keys/workers first, offer a scoped encrypted archive export of retained documents/consent proof/notice/DPA receipts + blob digests, record authorised delivery, then delete processor-held operational data per AVV instructions and documented backup expiry. Legal retention normally belongs to the controller; ACP does not invent its own indefinite archive duty. Controller-requested retention service needs a defined restricted contract, expiry and access; export failure is surfaced before destructive purge. Restoring a backup replays tombstone/suppression/erasure events before read/send availability, so restore cannot resurrect consent or erased contacts. Actual GCS lock/lifecycle/Cloud SQL backup evidence is AUT-371/release-owner work; no infrastructure configuration changed or verified here.

`docs/deletion-policy.md` must gain CustomerConsent/contact-state/suppression/notice/DPA rules in implementation. All new models/fields join derived `tools/tenant-restore/` manifest and export/purge/schema verification; RESTRICT FKs preserve tombstones/evidence until authorised retention pass. Tenant purge orders outbox/public token refs and evidence/consent/contact points before customer; account actor deletion preserves necessary audit identity snapshot without an orphan tenant FK. Document the blob inventory and test restore through FK cycles if any are introduced. No new schema/deletion code in this PR.

## Controller/processor and transparency model

For workshop customer data the workshop/legal entity is controller and ACP acts as processor on documented instructions (Art 28); ACP's own account/billing/security processing has separately disclosed roles, not covered by a blanket workshop consent. Use a versioned AVV/DPA with processing subject/duration/nature/purpose, data/subject categories, instructions, confidentiality/security, rights/breach assistance, subprocessor authorisation, return/deletion, audit and transfer safeguards. Only an authorised tenant OWNER/ADMIN records acceptance; keep exact accepted bytes/identity/date. Acceptance does not certify a tenant's marketing choices.

ACP publishes a versioned **subprocessor register** (identity, function, processing locations, transfer/safeguard summary), makes it accessible to controllers, records changes and sends contractual advance-change notice to tenant contract contacts using a service template/outbox. Proposed notice period is 30 days before adding/replacing a processor (contract policy, not statutory fixed period), objection route and documented resolution per AVV. No vendor/region/DPA/SCC status is assumed from a library or provider name; existing AUT-362 provider decisions/DPA requirements remain. No live subprocessor notice is sent in this ticket.

Maintain Art 30(2) processor record by controller/category/subprocessor/transfer/security measure, with controller's Art 30 record template and responsibilities separately identified. Before intake collection, tenant supplies an Art 13 notice; Art 14 applies to indirect sources as applicable, with source/timing recorded rather than an automatic paper checkbox. Notice template must identify controller/contact/DPO where applicable, purposes/bases, recipients/transfers, retention, subject rights/complaint, required-vs-optional data/consequences and relevant automated decisions. The short email footer from AUT-587 only links to this complete notice. Customer rights requests route to the controller, ACP assists securely under the AVV; identity checks proportional, no disclosure to a guessed email. In-app request tracking can follow in the erasure ticket, not an unauthorised customer-data inbox.

## Verification required in implementing PRs

- Ledger: tenant/customer/contact/evidence/actor FK isolation, append-only sequence, revoked_at only in cessation event, duplicate idempotency key, stale client revision, correction/supersession and replay order; no timestamp backdating resurrects a grant.
- Multi-controller tenant A/B: A consent/soft-opt-in/notice/DPA cannot authorise B; A token/reconsent cannot revoke or clear B state; A objection does not erase B permission, while explicit shared transport invalidity blocks both. Wrong-controller evidence/source links fail. Changing a site's legal entity cannot retarget a queued row. Staff without controller/site access cannot read its proof. Ambiguous legacy true flag yields no duplicated grant; adding B never inherits A's compatibility flag.
- Migration: true flag→transactional CONTRACT source with unknown historical collection; false/invalid/missing→no grant; no marketing; race backfill vs withdrawal; all address/intake/import/bounce writers dual-write; old-writer fence and parity before reader cutover. New address/return to old/duplicate customer cannot inherit permission or bypass suppression.

Transactional email does not need marketing grant or an ECG service to operate.
- Outbox race: revoke before enqueue/claim/handoff, duplicate-customer/address fence, retry/admin replay/resend after withdrawal, in-flight/timeout-unknown boundary and stale worker lease; no contact replacement at retry, no real provider/RTR calls in CI. Invalidity recorded for old snapshotted address without clearing newly corrected email.
